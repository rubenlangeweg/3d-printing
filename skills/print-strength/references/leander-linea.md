# The Linea studies

Use the [published analysis notes](https://github.com/rubenlangeweg/3d-printing/blob/main/projects/leander-linea/analysis/README.md) and their linked numerical results as the historical record. Do not run old results against a new mesh while keeping the old model label.

The accepted shelf is V4. The earlier interactive 3D shelf viewer contains V2/V3 fixed bonded mounting results and excludes self-weight. The later V3/V4 comparison uses a rigid 13 mm rail, tilted 26° from vertical, with frictionless compression-only contact and self-weight. The user confirmed that the 13 mm opening fits. The downloaded furniture model's approximate 10 mm thickness was not used as a measurement.

The matched V3/V4 comparison uses 2 mm voxels and 300 g and 2 kg front and corner loads. V4 has no independently refined 1 mm solve in that package. The separate V3 fine-grid check must not be attributed to V4. V4 has greater movement despite lower solid volume, so its deeper ribs were not proven equal in stiffness to V3.

The original organizer was studied with its top lip fixed in all directions, a finite floor load and self-weight, in its mounted orientation. Its 1 mm result was compared with 1.25 mm. Its fixed mounting is different from the shelf's separating contact. The imposed floor load assumes contents stay in place and transmit floor shear. A tall bottle's wall load and sliding require another case.

The historical reference was solid isotropic PLA, E 2.06 GPa, Poisson ratio 0.35 and density 1,240 kg/m³. The cited PLA Basic datasheet specimens used 100% infill and conditioning. For the user's PLA Matte shelf printed at 25% infill, these values remain a sensitivity reference. A warped print also differs from the ideal geometry and snug contact.

Keep the actual Linea table visible when making project product renders. Technical setup diagrams and analysis heatmaps may isolate the accessory when that explains the calculation. The downloaded furniture source and its textures stay local under their original terms.
